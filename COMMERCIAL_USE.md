# ABDS Commercial Use and Partnership Policy

ABDS is intended to be reviewable, testable, and discussable without granting automatic commercial production rights or allowing third parties to present protected ABDS materials as their own proprietary technology.

This document explains the commercial path. `LICENSE.md`, `PROVIDER_EVALUATION_LICENSE.md`, and the referenced standard licences control actual permissions.

## Public-use model

### Specification and documentation

ABDS v0.7.1-and-later specification and documentation materials are generally made available under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** unless a file says otherwise.

That public licence supports qualifying non-commercial study, review, teaching, research, critique, and adaptation subject to its terms, including attribution and ShareAlike. It does not grant commercial use.

### Software and executable tooling

ABDS software and executable implementation materials first published under the v0.7.1 framework are generally made available under **PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0** unless a file says otherwise.

A commercial organization, including a small startup, does not receive production or commercialization rights merely because of its size.

### Provider evaluation

A commercial organization may use the additional, limited rights in `PROVIDER_EVALUATION_LICENSE.md` to conduct internal technical evaluation and non-production proof-of-concept work for a limited period.

The evaluation licence is not a production licence.

## Activities that normally require a separate commercial agreement

Subject always to the exact applicable licence and mandatory law, the following are examples of activities for which NeuroSync expects a commercial party to obtain separate written permission where protected ABDS materials are used:

- deploying an ABDS implementation in a live or customer-facing product;
- offering an ABDS-derived API, gateway, SDK, platform, hosted service, or commercial feature;
- incorporating protected ABDS materials into a commercial AI-provider, model-router, cloud, billing, identity, or developer-platform integration;
- selling, licensing, sublicensing, white-labelling, or commercially redistributing protected ABDS software or documentation;
- packaging protected ABDS material as proprietary technology in a commercial proposal;
- using protected ABDS materials as the subject matter of a bid, licensing transaction, provider deal, acquisition proposal, investment solicitation, joint venture, or other commercial intermediation in which the third party presents itself as the technology owner or licensor;
- operating a commercial certification or conformance business using ABDS branding or protected test materials;
- removing required attribution and then marketing substantially copied protected materials as independently originated; or
- granting another party rights that the intermediary itself does not hold.

These examples do not extend copyright to abstract ideas, methods, systems, or other unprotected subject matter. They explain how the project's public licences and commercial path are intended to operate around **protected ABDS materials**.

## No automatic royalty formula

ABDS does not impose a public percentage-of-profit or percentage-of-revenue formula.

A fixed public royalty can create ambiguity about accounting, attribution, and provider adoption. Instead, commercial consideration is negotiated case by case and may reflect the value, scope, scale, exclusivity, territory, implementation work, and strategic relationship involved.

## Commercial arrangements

Possible arrangements include:

- a commercial implementation licence;
- a provider-native implementation licence;
- a provider design partnership;
- paid implementation, integration, architecture, or security work;
- support and maintenance;
- conformance tooling or certification services if established later;
- sponsorship of standards work;
- a revenue-, usage-, transaction-, or milestone-linked licence;
- a field-, product-, territory-, or time-limited exclusive licence;
- strategic investment;
- acquisition or assignment of specified rights; or
- another negotiated agreement.

NeuroSync may choose different commercial terms for different organizations or use cases. Public availability of ABDS does not create a most-favoured-customer obligation or require equivalent terms for different licensees.

## Provider design partners

AI Providers, model routers, cloud platforms, identity platforms, billing platforms, and developer platforms are invited to evaluate ABDS and engage as design partners.

A design partnership may focus on:

- Provider-authoritative entitlement resolution;
- OAuth and consent architecture;
- delegated grant semantics;
- Provider-side metering and accounting;
- user-visible limits and revocation;
- sponsored or organization-funded use;
- streaming, multimodal, batch, or agentic settlement;
- threat modelling and abuse prevention;
- interoperability testing; and
- reference implementation work.

A Provider's review of ABDS does not automatically grant NeuroSync access to that Provider's systems, and it does not imply Provider adoption. Likewise, repository access or technical discussion does not grant the Provider production or ownership rights beyond the applicable licence.

## Attribution, origin, and chain of title

ABDS should be referenced truthfully as a project originated by **Marc Johnston** and stewarded by **NeuroSync AI Dynamics (Pty) Ltd**.

A third party that wishes to commercialize an ABDS-derived implementation should be able to demonstrate a clean chain of rights for the protected material it uses. A party cannot convey greater copyright or trade mark rights than it actually holds.

NeuroSync preserves Git history, version records, contributor terms, and provenance notices to make the project's origin and rights chain auditable.

## No implied endorsement or certification

No organization may describe itself or a product as **ABDS Certified**, **Official ABDS**, **ABDS Approved**, **ABDS Partner**, or endorsed by NeuroSync merely because it has read, evaluated, forked, or implemented concepts from the repository.

No implementation may claim that a named AI Provider has adopted ABDS without evidence from that Provider.

See `TRADEMARKS.md`.

## Historical versions

Earlier ABDS versions were publicly described under different licensing positions.

- Through v0.6, repository materials were described as MIT licensed.
- The initial v0.7 framework described executable software under PolyForm Small Business License 1.0.0.
- The v0.7.1 framework changes new software and modifications prospectively to PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0.

The current framework does not attempt to revoke rights already validly granted under earlier applicable terms.

A commercial party relying on historical rights should identify the exact commit, file, version, and licence on which it relies. New v0.7.1-and-later modifications are not automatically covered by historical permissions.

## Sponsorship and voluntary support

Organizations and individuals may voluntarily support ABDS research and maintenance through sponsorship, grants, donations, engineering contributions, cloud credits, or other agreed assistance.

Voluntary support does not itself confer certification, exclusivity, ownership, Provider status, or broader commercial rights unless a separate written agreement says so.
